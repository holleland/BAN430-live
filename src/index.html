The October average is scored on 31 October (absolute error of the point forecast, and CRPS). Until then this is a projection, not a score.

How the scoring works

Each group submitted a point forecast and 19 quantiles (5%, 10%, …, 95%) for the nine days 23–31 October and for the October average. Lower scores are better.

CRPS is the average over the 19 quantiles of the quantile score (the sum of the quantile scores divided by 20): it rewards forecasts that are both close to the outcome and honest about their uncertainty. RMSE is the root mean squared error of the point forecasts. Bias is the average forecast error (positive: the forecasts were too high). 90% hits is how many outcomes fell inside the group's 5%–95% interval (about 9 in 10 is the ideal).

The official rule is that the last submission before the deadline (22 October, 12:00) counts, and the winner is the group with the best combined rank in four categories: daily RMSE, daily CRPS, and the absolute error and CRPS of the October average. This page ranks on the first two while the month is running, using the days known so far. Best submission of each group picks, in hindsight, the submission with the lowest CRPS so far. It is for fun and learning (it shows what the earlier forecasts were worth), and it is not the competition result.

The benchmarks (naive, seasonal naive and 7-day mean) are made on the deadline day with the same data, so you can see who beat them.

About the numbers. Scores are computed on the daily NO5 prices (NOK/kWh, excluding VAT) from hvakosterstrommen.no, which publishes tomorrow's prices at about 13:00. The page updates itself. The benchmark forecasts are made with the data available on the deadline day, with uncertainty from 2000 simulated paths that resample each model's past errors.
The page shows the group names that the groups chose for their submission, not the names of the members.
